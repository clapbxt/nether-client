# Friends, parties and world invites

Play with friends who also use Nether Client. You need to be signed in with Microsoft.

## Friends

Open **Friends** from the launcher's top bar, the Home page, or the Nether menu in game.

- **Add a friend** by their Minecraft name. They need Nether Client too; they'll see your request in their launcher or
  in game.
- The **Friends** tab shows who is online and what they're playing. Depending on what they share you can **Join** them,
  **Ask** to join, or **Invite** them.
- **Requests** shows requests you've received and sent. **Blocked** lists players you've blocked. Blocked players
  can't send you requests or invites, and aren't told.

Your friends settings let you choose whether friends see the server or world you're in, whether anyone can send you
requests, whether you get pop-ups (with Do not disturb), or go **Invisible**.

**Privacy:** our friends service stores who your friends are and your status. It never stores your messages, and there
is no chat in the friends list. More in [Privacy](../../PRIVACY.md).

## Parties

A party is your group on the Nether title screen. You and your friends stand together on the stage in your own skins,
capes and cosmetics, with name plates, a crown for the leader, and ready and mic indicators. Empty spots let you invite
friends.

- Invite friends, or accept their invites, from the party drawer. Anyone in the party can invite their own friends;
  the leader approves join requests.
- **Party chat** works on the title screen only. It never goes into a world or a server's chat.
- **Voice**: talk to your party, with per-person volume. Voice goes directly between party members and is never stored.
- The **leader** picks what to play (a singleplayer world or a server from their lists), everyone readies up, and a
  short countdown starts it for the whole party.
- If the leader leaves, the party passes to whoever joined next.

Nothing about a party is kept once it ends.

## Inviting friends into your singleplayer world

While you're in a singleplayer world, press **Invite** on the pause screen (or the game's own **Open to LAN**
button) and pick friends. There's nothing to set up: no port forwarding and no server. Your friend's launcher gets the
invite, starts a matching profile (same version and loader) and drops them straight into your world. If they have no
matching profile, it offers to make one. A friend whose game is already running can join from the invite card.

Friends connect to you directly when they can, and through a relay when a direct link isn't possible.
